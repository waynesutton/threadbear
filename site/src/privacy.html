Privacy

The short version: I don't collect anything about you, apart from anonymous crash reports. Your threads stay on your Mac and in a Convex deployment that's yours.

This website

threadbear.mikecann.app doesn't use cookies, analytics or ads. It's hosted on Cloudflare, which sees your internet address like any web host does and keeps its request logs for a few days so I can spot problems.

The Mac app

Threadbear runs on your Mac. It starts the official Claude Code and Codex command line tools, logged in as you, so what you send an agent goes to Anthropic or OpenAI under your own account and their own privacy policies. Threadbear never reads or copies your login tokens. It does read which account each login is and the token counts in your transcripts, to show how much room each has left.

Full transcripts, tool output and images stay on your Mac. A photo you send from your phone passes through your Convex deployment on its way there, with its location and other metadata removed, and is deleted once your Mac has it. Each thread's title and status, the text of its messages, a line for each tool it used, and anything waiting on you go up to your own Convex deployment, so your phone and browser can show them. None of it comes to me. The full list of what Threadbear installs and talks to is in on-your-mac.md.

There's also an activity log, for people working on Threadbear itself, which notes what you click and open in the app so an agent can see how you use it. It's off unless you turn it on in Settings, it never notes what you type, and it stays on your Mac, where each day's file is deleted after 30 days.

The iPhone app and thread links

The iPhone app and the browser links read the same Convex deployment, using a sign in code that only you can make from your Mac. You can cancel one at any time and that phone or browser stops working straight away.

The iPhone app only ever talks to the deployment you pair it with. The sign in code is kept in the phone's Keychain and the deployment's address in the app's settings, and nothing else about you is kept or sent anywhere. There's no account with me, no analytics, no ads and no tracking, and apart from crash reports (below) I don't collect anything from the app.

If you turn on notifications, Apple gives the phone a notification token, and the app saves it in your deployment so it can tell you when a thread needs you. The notifications go from your deployment through Apple's push service, and turning them off in the iPhone's Settings stops them.

Apple only delivers notifications for this app when they're signed with my developer key, so if your deployment doesn't have a key of its own, it sends them through a small relay I run, the one piece of Threadbear that's shared. From the next version of the app, the phone makes its own secret key and gives it only to your deployment, which locks the notification's text with it before it goes to the relay. The relay passes it on to Apple without being able to read it, and the phone unlocks it when it arrives. The relay keeps no notification tokens and nothing from your threads, just a random id for your deployment, the version it's on and daily totals of notifications sent. You can turn the relay off in your deployment, though you won't get notifications then.

The camera is used to scan the pairing code, when that picture never leaves the phone, and to take a photo when you choose to send one to a thread. Photos you choose from your library are only read when you pick them. The microphone is only used for Ask, after you press its button or tap the mic in an Ask thread, when you tap Dictate on a new thread, and when you tap the mic beside Send to dictate a reply. Apple's speech recognition turns it into text, on the phone where it can, otherwise through Apple's servers under Apple's privacy policy.

The sample threads you can look around in before pairing are made up, and they stay on the phone.

Crash reports

Your copy of Threadbear on your Mac never sends me crash reports; only my own copy does. When the iPhone app crashes or hits an error, it sends an anonymous report to Sentry, which I use to find and fix bugs. A report says where in Threadbear's own code it went wrong, the app's version, and the kind of device and system it ran on. It doesn't include your threads, messages, files, logins or anything that says who you are, and it isn't used for tracking or ads.
